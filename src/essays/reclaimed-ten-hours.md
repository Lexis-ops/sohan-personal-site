A few months ago I sat down and actually tracked where my working week was going. The answer was uncomfortable: a huge share of it was spent re-writing the same kinds of emails, re-structuring the same kinds of documents, and re-explaining the same context to myself every time I picked a task back up.

None of that was thinking. It was overhead.

The fix wasn't a single clever trick — it was building three small, boring systems and sticking to them long enough for them to become invisible. This is a walkthrough of what those systems actually look like in practice, and why the time they returned went straight back into the parts of my work that were never replaceable in the first place.
